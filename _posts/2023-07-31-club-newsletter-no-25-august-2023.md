### I can blunder better than you can…

{% include image.html align="left" url="/assets/image_uploads/nl25-wmotb-short-beliavsky.jpg" description="Short v Beliavsky, Linares 1992: Black has just been checked with 57…f7-f6+" %}

The worst move on the board (WMOTB): you can find it for yourself in this notorious position from Short v Beliavsky, Linares (1992). Both players were GMs. Our Nigel has just been checked with 57…f7-f6+ and managed to find the only way to lose on the spot.

[WMOTB](http://streathambrixtonchess.blogspot.com/2007/01/worst-move-on-board.html) was initially an enjoyable series on the Streatham and Brixton Chess Blog (2006-2016. RIP) which some of our older readers may remember. Justin Horton, now living in Spain but still in touch with us, was the editor of the sequence of some 26 posts featuring various creative ways to lose, pulled off not only by GMs, but also by sometime S&BCC members and bloggers.

{% include image.html url="/assets/image_uploads/nl25-wmotb-horton.jpg" description="Justin Horton’s game from 2019: Black to play 36…Kf4" %}

Justin has reprised the series in the current issue of the prestigious *New in Chess* magazine. For serious practitioners of the exquisite blunder (and I count myself as one) his article helpfully discusses which evaluation mark should be given to the WMOTB: a ‘!’ or a ‘?’ (and if so, how many). It also includes some fresh examples including the corker played by Justin himself (with Black) in an internet game in 2019. Eschewing all false modesty, he adorns 36…Kf4 with a double!! It’s reassuring that you don’t have to be a super-GM like Nigel to play the WMOTB – we can all do it…

### County contesting

As mentioned in previous editions, all S&BCC members are eligible to play for Surrey in county matches. Actually, you can play for any county where you live, work, study or were born – though only for one in a season, and of course you must be an ECF member. Matches are played on Saturdays: home fixtures at Cheam; away at other county’s venues, with lifts usually available. There is a relaxed time control of G120 +10spm (plenty of time for your opponent to find the WMOTB at their leisure!). A number of S&BCC players can often be seen for Surrey - which has multi-board teams at all strength levels.

Surrey did rather well this season getting into the national finals for the Open (though they lost to Middlesex on a tie break), and in the U2050 (but losing to Warwickshire 7 v 9 (your Editor and James Fox played). Martin Dale, Alan Romeril and Richard Tillett have also turned out for lower rated county teams.

The Surrey v Middlesex national Open final on 1 July was notable for having Streatham players on both sides: John Hodgson and Robin Haldane on boards 13 and 15 for Surrey, and for Middlesex Venkat on 3, and Andrew Stone on 6. Usually, we see them all on the same side in our London League 1st team. But on whichever side, no quarter was given. Venkat played a nice attacking game with White, offering the c3 pawn (with 11. Bg2) in the position below to retain the two bishops, and the initiative.
