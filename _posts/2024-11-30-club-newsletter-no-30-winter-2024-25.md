Welcome to issue number 30 of our club newsletter.

We got into the newsletter habit during the Covid lockdown, when over-the-board (OTB) chess went into hibernation and we wanted to maintain contact with our members. We’ve been pumping newsletters out ever since, recently on a quarterly basis.

This is a bumper issue as there’s lots to catch up on. Already this season we’ve played over 35 OTB and online matches, and there are 66 still to come. There are club events coming up over the Christmas and New Year period and a new competition for club members is planned if there are enough entries. So lots to look forward to.

With this issue we are trialling a different design approach which we hope is to your liking. It is still a work-in-progress, so do let us have your comments. If you have design skills we’d love to hear from you – maybe you can help us improve it – and please feel free to suggest ideas and material for the next issue. The editorial team is Richard Tillett (who did most of the work on this issue), Martin Smith and Antony Hall. Email us with your comments (contact details are in the PDF newsletter).

### From the Chair: Meet the New Boss!

{% include image.html align="left" url="/assets/image_uploads/nl30-antony-hall.jpg" description="Antony Hall" %}

At our 2024 AGM Martin Smith stepped down as President and Antony Hall took on the new role of Chair. Antony knows his way around the chess scene – he is widely experienced as a player and organiser in club, league and county chess. Here’s what he has to say:

"Meet the new boss, same as the old boss," sang The Who in Won't Get Fooled Again. It’s a big challenge and, though I can’t promise to meet Martin Smith’s very high standards, I will certainly do my best!

For those of you who don't know me, I have been a member of S&BCC for almost 20 years. I have captained our London League second team for most of that time and have played for our London League first team (when they were desperate!) and our third team (when we ran one before Covid). I've also played for the club in some of the Surrey League competitions.

You may be wondering how I see my role as Chair, and how it differs from President. Possibly not by very much, but I’m learning as I bed myself in. It's really a case of "if it ain't broke, don't fix it". I have big shoes to fill: the club has been very well run in my time as a member and I aim to continue that, working with the other officers.

I don't think we're looking to change much, but we realise that many hands make light chess club administration so we're hoping to get more people involved, mostly in small ways, going forward. We are also looking at how we can ensure that we are offering sufficient opportunities for all our members to play chess, be that in the various leagues, our club night events, our online competitions and ‘specials’ such as inter-club one-day team rapidplays (two of which are planned for 2025).

### Watch Out, Christmas Is About

Continuing Antony’s musical theme, we’re all dreaming of a white Christmas. The white chess pieces that is, not the snow – we can do without that.

Our popular Christmas Blitz and New Year Rapid competitions will be back again at our St Thomas’s Church venue. Alan Romeril will be controller for both events. Club and library group members and their friends are welcome to enter. No entry fees, but be warned: we will be rattling the donations box!

The Christmas Blitz is on 10 December starting at 7:30pm: 7 rounds, time control 5 minutes per game with a 3 second increment each move. The New Year Rapid is on 7 January (Rounds 1-3) and 16 January (Rounds 4-6), also starting at 7:30pm: time control 25 minutes per game with no increment. Watch your email inbox for further details.

### A New Club Competition... Would You Enter?

For some months now we’ve been wondering whether we should hold a standardplay competition for our club and library members. Nothing unusual about that you might think – many if not most clubs have them. We’ve considered the idea in the past but never been confident that there would be sufficient interest from our members.

This season we want to give it a go. If we have enough entrants we will probably schedule the competition in the March to June period when there is capacity at our home venue and the league season is starting to run down, though it could start earlier.

Most games will be played at our St Thomas’s Church venue but to ensure that the arrangements are as flexible as possible we want to allow other venues, such as players’ homes. We haven’t yet decided on format, which will be determined once we have a clearer idea of the likely entrants. It could be run along the lines of the Summer Individuals competition organised by the Surrey County Chess Association where the entry is divided into several sections of similar strength players. We might have a section for Streatham Library players whose ratings are below the threshold for league chess but are ambitious and want to work towards their first ECF rating.

We will be contacting club and library players to assess interest, but by all means drop us an email if you think you might like to play or have any comments (contact details are in the PDF newsletter).

### AGM Highlights

Great to see some new faces at our Annual General Meeting on 31 August at Streatham Library. Lots of productive decision-making and discussion. First up there was the formal business of signing off the club accounts which are in good shape thanks to our Treasurer Paul Ritchie. Once again we have been able to leave our subscription rates unchanged, at £45 (full), £22.50 (concessionary) and £4.50 (game fee) which is where they have been since 2018. That’s some achievement when you take into account recent levels of inflation, and only possible thanks to our members’ generous donations.

We elected the key officers for the 2024/25 season (Antony Hall takes over from Martin Smith as Chair, while Paul continues as Treasurer and Richard Tillett soldiers on as Secretary for one final year).

Next we brainstormed a plan we are putting in place to improve the way we manage our club.

There’s a considerable To Do list and over the coming months we’ll be actively recruiting volunteers so we can spread the load further. Thanks to everyone who has already volunteered to help get the club onto a sound footing – all and any help appreciated!

Martin took over as President during the difficult Covid period and has steered us to a strong position since. He is still much involved with our club both as a player and hard-working volunteer, contributing to club initiatives and co-editing our newsletters.

### S&B in the Community – Our Library and Rookery Groups

Our Chess at Streatham Library group (Tuesdays 4:30-7:30) continues to prosper. Often we have 30 or more stopping by for some social chess, new faces as well as old, and every so often someone shows up who we haven’t seen for years. We’ve been around for so long now – around 20 years we think, no one is quite sure – that we’ve been able to make long-lasting relationships.

The format hasn’t changed much over the years, though we make incremental improvements and try out new ideas whenever we can, such as the WhatsApp group we recently set up so that we can get messages out easily and quickly to the core group. In the New Year we will be reviewing and updating our safeguarding policies.

This year Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve fall on Tuesdays so there will be no chess at Streatham Library on 24 December and 31 December.

We held two organised sessions this summer on the open-air chess tables by the Rookery Café on Streatham Common, led by club and library group members in conjunction with the Friends of Streatham Common and their game-whizz Mike Robinson, who has written a chess program. Alan Romeril, brave man, took it on at one of the sessions – the program won!

We have a WhatsApp group for the Rookery players so do put the word out if you’d like to meet up any time. Sets are always available from the Rookery Café for pop-up games.

### S&B in the Leagues – How Our Season Is Shaping Up

Over 100 over-the-board and online matches this season... and we are off to a flying start with most of our teams performing strongly. The main change this year is that we’ve entered a team in the Minor Division of the London League to give our growing cohort of under-1650 players more opportunities to play in our central London teams. Read on for more about how we’re shaping up in the leagues and visit our [club calendar](https://streathamchess.org/calendar/) to see our fixture schedule for the remainder of the season.

#### London Chess League

We’ve long had a strong presence in the upper echelons of the London League, but in recent years we have not been so well represented in the lower divisions. This season we’ve gone some way towards rectifying that and we now have one team in each of Divisions 1, 2 and the Minor. Our home matches are played at the London League’s Bethnal Green venue. Some of our away matches are also played there; others at our opponents’ central London venues.

Click [here](http://lms.englishchess.org.uk/lms/organisation/1267/tables) for the division tables and [here](http://lms.englishchess.org.uk/lms/organisation/1267/fixtures) for fixtures and results.

**Division 1:** Now under Graham Keane’s captaincy, our 8-board team started the season with a resounding 6-2 win over Mushrooms 1, but this was quickly followed by two losses, one of which was against Wood Green 1 with their customary line-up of formidable, mostly titled, players. We’ve returned to form now following a closely contested win against Hackney 1, which takes us to a points tally of 4 from a possible 8.

**Division 2:** Antony Hall’s 8-board team is off to a great start having won its two matches. "Unlike last season," he says, "we have actually managed to play two matches before Christmas! The opener last month, against Cavendish 2 (the only other second team in the division), saw three of our strongest players withdraw in the week of the match. That is bad luck! Despite this, and being significantly outrated, we edged a close match to record back-to-back wins against our opponents for the first time ever."

"Our second match against Albany saw us run out 5-3 winners, getting revenge for our defeat to them last season. Winning consecutive matches was something we failed to do last season and we are one of only two teams left on 100%."

**Minor Division:** Our new Minor team is also enjoying a great start to the season. This is a popular division with 13 4-board teams in contention. After four wins and one loss the team is currently topping the table, one point clear of the chasing pack. Team captain Ed Mospan is still building his player squad so let him know (contact details are in the PDF newsletter) if you’re not already receiving his emails and would like to be on his list (players have to be rated below 1650 ECF to be eligible for the team).

#### Central London Chess League

Most matches in the Central London League are played at Pimlico Chess Club’s popular venue in Pimlico. We have teams in Division 2 and Division 4, both of which performed strongly last season.

**Division 2:** Will our 5-board first team win promotion to Division 1 this season? It’s looking good: we’re currently topping the table after three wins and a draw from four matches, two clear points ahead of Battersea Galacticos who have also played four matches.

Click [here](http://oxfordfusion.com/oca/GenerateTable.cfm?LeagueID=474&Org=7) for the Division 2 table and match results.

Some thoughts from team captain Andrew Stone:

"Last year we missed out on promotion, by the narrowest of margins, to the very strong OTB Gnomes (both teams would have likely challenged to win Division 1 if they were in that division). This season we do not face the same strong challenge, but Hammersmith have considerable resources and Battersea have brought in two strong players. Additionally the rules (or lack of) accommodate using first (and second) team players so we must be wary of Pimlico 3 who, although they cannot get promoted (maximum of two teams per division from the same club), may target us in individual matches."

**Division 4:** Last season Paul Ritchie’s 4-board team won the division though it didn’t receive its just reward of promotion to Division 3 (we don’t make the rules!). This season has been tougher and after four matches we have lost two and won two, but with six matches to come we can still claw our way back into contention.

Click [here](http://oxfordfusion.com/oca/GenerateTable.cfm?LeagueID=477&Org=7) for the Division 4 table and match results.

#### Croydon & District Chess League

This season we are again fielding four teams in the Croydon League and we have one team in each of the four standardplay divisions. The upper rating ceilings for the four-board teams are: Open, no limit; Division 2, 7800; Division 3, 6600; and Division 4 5800.

Just two matches played so far this season, against HMRC Croydon in Division 4 and South Norwood in Division 2, and we won them both. Click [here](https://lms.englishchess.org.uk/lms/organisation/1250) for the Croydon League results website.

12-year old Charlie Pearson played a near flawless game in the match against HMRC Croydon. You can see it [here](https://www.chess.com/analysis/game/pgn/2d3mhSC5KC?tab=analysis) on Lichess.

There are 19 Croydon League matches still to come this season so plenty of opportunities to play. Our team manager is Richard Tillett – let him know (contact details are in the PDF newsletter) if you’re not already on his squad list and would like to be.

#### Alexander Cup

Oh dear, knocked out in the first round. Again. By the same club as last year, Kingston. And in spite of putting out a strong team.

The Alexander Cup is the flagship knock-out competition organised by the Surrey County Chess Association for clubs in Surrey and South West London. Although we lost 3.5-6.5, we put on a sturdy performance which included excellent wins for Phil Makepeace and Andrew Ponting. You can navigate to the match card [here](https://www.scca.co.uk/) via the SCCA website, or read Stephen Moss’s very readable account of the match with photos [here](https://kingstonchess.com/new-look-kingston-team-triumph-at-streatham/) (Stephen is a Guardian journalist and author of The Rookie about his experiences taking up chess).

#### 4NCL Online

We have two teams in this competition, one in Division 1 and the other in Division 3. Our team manager Matt Tillett selects teams from a squad of 15 players. The current competition (Season 10) will be ending soon but let him know (contact details are in the PDF newsletter) if you would like to be added to his squad for Season 11 which starts in January 2025.

The Four Nations Chess League introduced this competition during the pandemic when OTB chess was a no-no. It proved popular, and is still going strong although OTB has long since resumed. Two competitions are played each year to online standard time controls (45 mins + 10 sec increments). Matches are played alternate Tuesdays during the season starting at 7:30 pm.

It’s great entertainment watching the games play out on Lichess in real time – just go to the 4NCL Online website [here](https://4ncl.co.uk/data/onlines10/online_results_10.htm) and navigate to the Results, Pairings and Tables page.

**Division 1:** We have a strong squad, which took us to the semi-final stage in Season 9, but this season we’ve struggled for points. With six matches played we have five points from a possible 12 and a mid-table position in Group A, so no semi-finals for us.

**Division 3:** Just four points from a possible 12 and we’re currently propping up the table with one match left to play. Matches have mostly been closely contested and lots of interesting chess played.

### Just Tested: Puzzle Solution

At the diagram there is a mate in 14, or so Stockfish tells us after a little think, starting with 31.Qe5+. No chance anyone would work that out in real time against the clock. But congrats if you did from the comfort of your own screen. Lord Crazy played 31.Qg5+, and went down in flames. Matt takes up the story "His mistake was allowing my king to go forwards to the pawn (moves 31-32). I thought this was a mistake at the time. I could see that he had a perpetual check, but I had not seen the forced checkmate. I suspect he was trying too hard for the win and underestimated how it would turn if I got my king to the pawn in the corner."
