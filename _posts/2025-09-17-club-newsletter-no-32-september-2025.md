### Upbeat AGM looks forward to the upcoming season…

Ten members attended the AGM on the 30 September – the best attended for some years. Antony Hall (Chair) kicked off things off by saluting Richard Tillett’s ‘retirement’ after 24 years as Club Secretary and presenting him with a bug (of the creepy crawly kind) identification guide - it’s what he does when he is not organising chess, though fat chance given the demands of the exciting new Club Night start-up detailed below.

And in with the New! Welcome to Chris Bernard, our new Club Secretary: a seasoned chess organiser and tournament addict (and ghost of Crystal Palace CC). Read all about him in our interview below. The AGM confirmed the Club’s core management of Chris plus Chair Antony Hall and Treasurer Paul Ritchie – a great team to take us into the 2025-26 season supported by a growing band of volunteers detailed in Appendix 1.

And hello, too, to ‘Chess at the Café’ club nights! An ancient club tradition will make a welcome return under the enthusiastic leadership of Ed Mospan (you’ll have seen him at the Library Group – and at Kingston CC if you’ve been over that way). He and Richard will be nudging this initiative along – with lots of innovative ideas for social, fun, and rated, chess on Tuesday evenings at St. Thomas’s Café.

**Season Opener Blitz: Tuesday 30 September**

* Venue: St Thomas' Café, St Thomas' Church, Telford Avenue, SW2 4XW. Starting at 7.30pm prompt. If you arrive late you may default your first-round game.
* 7 rounds. Players will be paired with others on the same or a similar score (Swiss pairings). 5 minutes per game plus a 3 second increment each move.
* All games blitz rated by the English Chess Federation (ECF). You don't need to be a member of the ECF to play in this event.
* Ed Mospan is organising the event and Alan Romeril will be our controller on the night. To enter, contact Ed (his details are in the PDF newsletter).

### … and looked back at successes in 2024-25…

Chair Antony Hall reviewed the past season. There were standout performances from our OTB London League MI, Croydon League D and both our Central London League teams. We did well, too, in one-off team events (Surrey County Thorp – 2nd place by our U1450s; SCCU London Club Champs – 1st place by our U1650s). *Team photo in the PDF, left to right: Tom Mayers, Nic Basty, Ed Mospan, Austin Sopocko, Richard Tillett.*

There was also success in the 4NCL Online where our Division 4 side reached the semi-final (only to be knocked out on board count, though they did win promotion to Division 3). For the full details of our 2024-25 league season see Appendix 2. The club’s occasional blitz and rapidplay events at St Thomas’s Café continued to prove popular (see full report below), as did the rapidplay initiative started by Ed Mospan at the Tuesday Streatham Library Group. We supported ‘Chess in the Community’, not only via the thriving Library Group, but also the outdoor chess table meet-ups at Ruskin Park and Streatham Common. Throughout the season, several S&BCCers were out and about in congresses across the UK (with an impressive club presence at Kingston in August – see separate note below); and represented five counties (in two as captain) (see Appendix 3). Antony rounded off by reflecting on the great progress made over the season across many areas of club management, building the impressive team of officers and volunteers detailed in Appendix 1.

Treasurer Paul Ritchie reported that the club’s balance sheet showed a small surplus, and that subs could remain at the same level for another year – another success! However, he highlighted that the Club’s reliance on donations (from a small number of donors) carried some risks. Both subs levels and donor dependency may have to be addressed at the next year’s AGM.

The roster of teams and captains for the 2025-26 season - showing additional teams in the Central London and Croydon Leagues and a turnover of some captains – is detailed in Appendix 4. The Player of the Year was declared as Charlie Pearson – see the separate POTY report below.

### Meet Chris Bernard: From Ghost of Crystal Palace to S&BCC Secretary

We’re delighted to welcome our new Secretary Chris Bernard as the new public face of Streatham & Brixton Chess Club—although in the world of South London chess, Chris is hardly a stranger.

"I started playing club chess after my son was born," Chris tells us. "First with Beckenham & Bromley, then Crystal Palace. When the Palace sadly closed its doors, I made my way to S&BCC."

A regular on the congress circuit, Chris has a taste for travel. "Apparently, I like small islands—I’ve played in Kos, Guernsey, Reykjavik, various parts of Crete, and once even in a tournament in Condom, France," he laughs. His most recent outing was the August Thanet Congress. Despite now flying S&BCC’s colours in the London and Croydon Leagues, Chris is part of a group of former Crystal Palace players who still register under the old club’s name at congresses. “We call ourselves the Ghosts of Crystal Palace. So don’t be offended if you don’t see the S&BCC flag next to my name at some events!”

Off the board, Chris is no less active. He’s contributed significantly to the wider chess community through various administrative roles: Secretary of the Croydon Chess League, Kent County Chess Association, and the Southern Counties Chess Union. He also captains the Kent U2050 team. His dedication to the game has now brought him into a key role at S&BCC: "I offered my time to act as Secretary of the club. I’m looking forward to helping where I can."

Please join us in welcoming Chris—player, organiser, and ghostbuster—to the Streatham & Brixton Chess Club family.

### Club tournaments

Great turn outs again for the Club’s end of season internal tournaments at St. Thomas’s Café. The July 1 Blitz saw a five-way pile-up at the top of the table when Robin Haldane, Charlie Pearson, Martin Smith, Amar Adatia, and Austin Sopocko all finished on 5/7. 18 players slugged it out under Alan Romeril’s smooth tournament control. Generous donations covered the cost of the evening.

A two-evening rapid, spread over 15 and 22 July, attracted 21 contestants (with 14 playing both evenings). Peter Lalic beat the field with a clean 6/6 sweep, well clear of Martin Smith and Tom Mayers following up on 4 each. Once again Alan ran the event with his customary aplomb and efficiency. At both events new faces were made welcome alongside club regulars. (The tables are linked in the PDF newsletter.)

### Player of the Year 2024-25 – report by Martin Smith

As usual we had an impressive short list for the annual Player Of The Year: notable for its range of playing strength (from fourth team level to first) and from junior to senior. Martin Dale (a senior, and another CPCC ghost) was mentioned in despatches for his reliability in the Croydon League; Andrew Ponting was on our short list for scoring highest (and playing most games) for the London League 2nds and notching up a modest rating gain to boot. The junior Han brothers, Qixiang and Qixuan, might have been joint POTY’s in any other year: they both racked up impressive rating gains over the season, and impressed with their performances in the London League and their enthusiasm for tournament play (each played over 50 rated OTB games in the season). We were spared the invidious task of choosing between them as we were delighted to declare another promising junior, Charlie Pearson, as this year’s POTY. The citation includes his massive 500 odd rating point gain this year, his willingness to turn out for the Croydon and London Leagues, and his fantastic contribution to our Online effort scoring 6/7 in the last season. Charlie has also impressed in our internal club tournaments, at County level, and has travelled the length and breadth of the country (well over 60 OTB games) to gain two norms to qualify for the England Junior Squad.

**Congratulations Charlie – S&BCC Player Of The Year 2024/25.**

### Kingston – we came, we played, but we didn’t conquer – Graham Keane (another ghost) reports

In August, four club members played in the [Kingston Invitational Swiss](https://kingstonchess.com/kingston-invitational/fourth-kingston-invitational-12-16-august-2025/) run by Kingston CC: Phil Makepeace, Qixiang and Qixuan Han and myself, playing in my first 9-round tournament for over 50 years. The tournament was well organised with good playing space, air conditioning (it was 30 degrees outside!) and an endless supply of soft drinks and donuts… Ed Mospan of this parish was one of the efficient arbiters - and earned his first FIDE Arbiters norm.

I scored just 4/9, though I did manage one good win\* (all games marked \* can be played through at the end of this section) after repeating the first twelve moves from the previous day. First time I lost to Qixiang - fortunately, my second opponent didn’t play nearly as well!

> **Murder on the Black Squares.** Graham (black) threatens mayhem on the a7-g1 diagonal. His opponent tried to dodge with 23 Bg5. Can you work out Graham’s forcing sequence to finish the game\*?

The other three Streatham players had very successful tournaments. Qixuan continued his recent big improvement. All his opponents had higher ratings, yet he scored 5 points including wins over experienced players Bob Eames\* and Clive Frostick. Brother Qixiang was in contention for the junior prize until his last round loss, but his score of 50% was excellent – including that win\* over the Streatham London League First Team captain: he gained a significant number of rating points.

Phil played very well, and his win\* against IM Jack Rudd (one of the two IMs he beat) was typical of his accurate play. He was in contention for 3rd place until his final round loss to a WGM. Playing alongside, in a separate all-play-all IM event, was Peter Lalic. Although the lowest rated player he scored an impressive 3.5/9 including a 110-mover\* (typical!) which held up the next round until a draw was finally agreed.

### CheckMate Retreats for Streatham and Brixton Chess Club – from Gabriel Barr

After several years wrangling ‘light’ topics like Brexit and Covid, it was time to hang up my Department for Health Lenovo laptop and take a sabbatical to Brazil. There I reconnected with family on my mum's side, polished my Portuguese at language school, organised chess sessions and tournaments for youths in the picturesque Pantanal wetlands, taught English in Rio favelas, and attended a 10-day silent retreat among other adventures.

Along the way, an idea began to crystallise: chess-based retreats. Countless conversations with my good friend and National Master, Sam Walker, shaped this into CheckMate Retreats – mindful getaways in the peaceful countryside, combining high-quality chess coaching with yoga, meditation, and digital detox.

Whether you’re a casual player or seasoned club competitor, our retreats offer the chance to elevate your game in a restorative fun-filled environment with fellow chess enthusiasts. Our first weekend is 21-23 November. If you’re curious, or know someone who might be, you can find out details of venue, cost, etc [here](https://linktr.ee/checkmateretreats).
