### Chair’s Progress Report – Antony Hall

Our various teams in the London, Central London, Croydon & District and 4NCL Online Leagues have all been busy since the last newsletter (full reports below), and several members have been active for their respective county teams (also reported below).

The Club ran its Christmas Blitz (fast becoming a tradition) in December, and New Year Rapidplay over two Tuesdays in January: both at St Thomas’ Café, and both a great success (reports below). There has been much discussion amongst the club officers about running an internal standard play competition of some form but, ultimately, we’ve decided it’s not something we could do this season. We are, however, hopeful of running something next season, so watch this space.

By the time you read this, three teams will have played in the Thorp Trophy competition, a team Rapidplay which has been revived by the SCCA. More from Martin Smith below! We are also hoping to run three teams in the SCCU/ London Club Championships in June. Ed Mospan will be organising our entry this year and has already sent out a call for interest. I played in this last year, and it was great fun, so I encourage you to give it a go.

Our secretary, Richard Tillett – held in the highest esteem by us all – had intended to step back from his duties at the end of this season. However, he has been forced to bring this forward with immediate effect, although he will continue to assist with some of his many responsibilities while we look work out how to replacement him (whole, or in several parts!). Richard has been a stalwart for S&BCC for many years and will be a huge loss. A more fulsome tribute appears below. Not unrelated to Richard's departure is the fact that we are working to get more people involved to spread the work of running the club.

### Richard Tillett – A Tribute by Antony Hall and Martin Smith

{% include image.html url="/assets/image_uploads/nl31-richard-tillett.jpg" description="Richard Tillett" %}

Richard Tillett's involvement with S&B has been longstanding and multi-faceted. Our comprehensive club history, Movers and Takers, says that he was first listed as a member in 1991 - becoming involved with the club with his wife Laila and son Matthew (now a strong player for us in the London League, and captain of our 4NCL Online teams).

Richard became club secretary and treasurer in 2002, taking over from Chris Baker and Andy Thake respectively and, as Movers and Takers states: ‘He brought flair to both portfolios, combining administrative acumen with strategic vision. He safeguarded the club's financial viability in challenging times, while providing a sense of direction through the tangle of issues concerning subscription levels.’

Richard redesigned the club website in 2007. Then and since he has grappled with the challenges and opportunities of the digital age so as to enhance the club’s impact and improve its efficiency. He was treasurer until Paul Ritchie took over in 2019 and has continued as a key member of the club's management team over many years. He resigned as secretary in 2014 to focus on a wider development role, notably with the Library Group (whose potential he saw: guiding it through sticky patches and generously investing in it considerable time and energy). In recent years Richard has again stepped back into a secretarial role.

He had also been a contributor to the very successful [S&B Chess Blog](https://streathambrixtonchess.blogspot.com/) (which closed in 2016) demonstrating tenacious sleuthing skills in an [epic 25-episode, 4 year-long blog series](https://streathambrixtonchess.blogspot.com/2006/10/every-picture-tells-story-index.html) (jointly with Martin Smith) researching and tracking down a lost early 19th Century chess painting.

For many years Richard served the Surrey County Chess Association: both as a member of its Appeals Committee and organiser of its correspondence teams. He also has been a key office holder and Chair, for as long as anyone can remember, of the Croydon and District Chess League, in which he lately managed all four of our teams.

As our current secretary, Richard has been the 'front door' to the club, fielding a constant flow of enquiries and demands. He became the lynchpin of the club's administration, with a purposeful strategic overview and leadership. He possesses a great talent for persuasive diplomacy – or diplomatic persuasion! His are big shoes to fill – and, realistically, we will need to reconfigure the administrative workload to make it manageable in the future. We are grateful to Richard for being so helpful in smoothing the way to these new arrangements.

Our heartfelt thanks go to Richard for his untiring devotion and work for the club. His stepping down as a club officer is a great loss, and we hope that he will continue to support us in a more modest management role. We also hope, above all, that he will now have more time to enjoy his chess.

**Minor Division – Ed Mospan.** Our 4-board team in the Minor Division (for players up to 1650) of the London League has gelled very well. The best aspect of playing in this League and division is the opportunity for over the board self-improvement, time management, tactics, and seat of your pants action, all of which come with competitive match games - and a 9:30 end time allowing for post-game refreshment and analysis. For improving players, it is the perfect London League division to start out in. #### [4NCL Online](https://4ncl.co.uk/data/onlines11/online_results_11.htm) – Matt Tillett

S&B once again entered the 4NCL Online competition, which is now in its 11th season, having started during Covid with two seasons per year. Games are played bi-weekly on the LiChess platform, ECF rated, on a 45 minute plus 15 second time control. It's a pleasant way to get some proper rated Chess from the comfort of your own home. We entered two four board teams into the competition. The A team plays in the top division, where ratings range from around 1800 at the low end all the way up to 2400 on some of the teams' top boards. After six rounds, the A team is holding its own against strong opposition and is currently mid table with one round to play. The B team, playing in the 4th division, is currently in second place, with a shot at reaching the playoffs if we can win our last match. Ratings in the 4th division typically range from 1300 up to 1800. We have struggled in one or two rounds to field a full B team, so if anyone not currently in the squad is interested, please let me know and we can get you involved for the next season.

### Not Getting Enough Rated Standardplay?

The energetic Ed Mospan, who runs our LL Minor team, and who is also active at Kingston Chess Club, is the centre of a great local inter-club initiative: to offer rated games at club venues outside the usual league/match framework. We call them ‘rated friendlies’ for want of a better description, though they’re not all that ‘friendly’ as they are played under strict competitive conditions and to the same rules as the league matches that typically take place at the same time. There is no charge to play, and results are submitted for grading via the League Management System in the usual way by the clubs hosting the games.

Ed has been spreading the word at S&B, Kingston, and Epsom clubs, all of whom are participating, and he has other clubs in his sights as well. So far this initiative is mostly being taken up by lower rated players, who often have fewer opportunities to play in league teams; however players of all strengths are welcome. Just let Ed know if you fancy a game and he will pair you against a suitable opponent. Game nights, when they happen, are Tuesdays (S&B), Mondays (Kingston). Ed can be contacted by email (contact details are in the PDF newsletter).

### Chess in the Community Boom! Chess at Streatham Library Is Flourishing

*An update from Richard Tillett*

Community chess is already a Big Thing in South London and it just keeps on getting Bigger, fuelled by publicity such as [this video](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/videos/cd7d27wpqz5o) on BBC News from January.

Our Chess at Streatham Library group is benefiting from the boom, though we had a couple of hiccups this year due to refurbishment work at Streatham Library depriving us of access to the community hall. Not surprisingly, attendances were down during our temporary relocations, but numbers quickly recovered once we were able to return.

We launched a new initiative on 11 March, which Ed Mospan and I had been planning for a while but had to postpone because of the relocation. The idea was to offer library group members the opportunity to play rated rapidplay games during the Tuesday sessions, on a similar basis to the ‘rated friendlies’ Ed organises at other clubs (as mentioned above). We decided to allocate up to five boards for the rapidplays, situated at one end of the hall and separated from the rest of the group. Ed is to act as controller and organise the pairings in advance, retaining some flexibility on the day. Two rounds of rapidplay per session scheduled with an option to squeeze in an additional round when games finish quickly. Results to be submitted for rating the following day. What could possibly go wrong? I held my breath… but I shouldn’t have worried as Ed had everything under control.

We’ve now had five weeks of the rapidplay games: 23 players have participated and have played a total of 132 games. You can see the results [here](https://lms.englishchess.org.uk/lms/organisation/1163) on the LMS. Thank you Ed, you’re doing a great job! The word has been getting around and the prospect of rated rapidplay games is bringing in new faces, some of whom I expect to see playing in our S&B league teams before long.

{% include image.html align="left" url="/assets/image_uploads/nl31-library-hall.jpg" description="A Tuesday session of the Chess at Streatham Library group" %}

By midway through the Tuesday sessions, we are usually at full capacity with 40 or more people in the room. What restricts us is not the space in the community hall – there’s plenty of that. The issue is shortage of tables – budget constraints at the London Borough of Lambeth are preventing the purchase of more tables. I do sometimes wonder how big we could be if we had more capacity but maybe 40 is just fine.

**Chess in the Fresh Air!** We hope to organise some Saturday sessions up at the Rookery chess tables now that the weather is warming up. In the meantime, a big shout out for the new Ruskin Park tables. David Marshall reports: there are two new chess tables in Ruskin Park with chess sets to borrow from the café. On Saturday 22 March there was a successful launch event with about 30 participants, including from the Streatham and Carnegie library groups, plus enthusiastic locals and passers-by. They are hoping to hold monthly ‘chess in the park’ mornings over the summer and create a WhatsApp group for players. [Editorial Note: John Ruskin, the eminent Victorian polymath, after whom the Park is named, lived locally, and was himself a keen student of the game.]
